## 1. Blocking — numeric v2 contradicts its frozen parent and leaves grammar choices open

**Quote (§4):** “at most one currency marker per answer (two → `unsupported_answer`, as v1)” and “Every other v1 behaviour is unchanged”.
**Finding:** The pinned v1 accepts `FINAL: Rs 5 rupees`, `FINAL: ₹5 INR` and `FINAL: 5 Rs INR`, even for gold value 5/unit apples (verified by a read-only probe). Global two-marker rejection would therefore change old successes, including non-rupee scores. Word boundaries, wrapper precedence, suffix combinations, `/−`-style notation and whether mismatching rupee values are `mismatch` or `unsupported_answer` also need fixed behavior.
**Amendment:** “Run v1 first and preserve every result unless its extraction status is `unsupported_answer`. Only then try a gold-blind extension, retaining v1's preprocessing, syntax checks, wrapper/assignment order, scalar grammar and suffix grammar. At the existing prefix slot additionally match case-insensitive `rupees` or `rupee` followed by whitespace, a digit, sign, decimal point or supported wrapper opener; consume following whitespace. Alternatively accept suffix exactly `₹` after stripping whitespace, immediately after the scalar/wrapper; no intervening word suffix. Reject new parses with another currency marker: an existing/new prefix, suffix symbol, or suffix word token casefolding to `rupee`, `rupees`, `inr`, `rs` or `rs.`. The old `/-` exception remains restricted to old prefixes. Accept only parses using a new path; otherwise return v1. Normalize gold unit by stripping whitespace and casefolding, then use the listed six-unit set. For non-rupee gold return v1; for rupee gold return `match` or `mismatch` by exact value. Preserve old two-marker successes. Freeze positive/negative fixtures for adjacency, assignments, nested wrappers, punctuation, multiple markers, non-rupee gold and mismatches; publish actual Pilot-B rescoring separately from the historical proxy.”

## 2. Blocking — zero-width bootstrap intervals manufacture certainty

**Quote (§7–8):** “the number of distinct d_i values and degeneracy”; “L ≤ 0 ≤ U and U < +5 (rules out the useful gain)”.
**Finding:** All `d_i=0` gives `[0,0]`, hence “informative null” and retention established automatically. Under rare 1-pp gains (`D=1` with probability .01), this occurs with probability `.99^100 = .366`: the interval misses the population mean, although ruling out a 5-pp gain is not itself wrong in that example. More directly, at the retention null boundary (`D=−1` with probability .02, otherwise 0), all-zero samples falsely establish retention with probability `.98^100 = .133`. Constant nonzero differences can likewise produce artificial certainty. Existing `research/conditional_calibration/README.md` documents sparse-data failure; logging it does not protect labels.
**Amendment:** “Before applying any primary or retention label, if all family contrasts are identical or the percentile interval has zero width, report `inconclusive—degenerate empirical interval`; suppress inferential retention/support claims. Preserve the point estimate, raw interval and bounded reference. For nondegenerate data retain the existing primary table unchanged. ‘Informative null’ rules out gains ≥5 pp under the stated approximate sampling model; it neither establishes equality nor excludes meaningful harm. Report the count of nonzero family contrasts and both bootstrap tails; do not switch interval methods after results.”

## 3. Should-fix — the family population and independence assumptions are missing

**Quote (§7):** “conditional on the 9 trained checkpoints and the unchanged model”.
**Finding:** Conditioning on checkpoints solves neither curated-benchmark representativeness nor shared-template/author dependence. The earlier conditional-inference contract explicitly required these choices. Wilson and Hoeffding intervals also need a sampling interpretation. GPT/Claude authoring with human repair is not a random sample of authors or Hinglish users.
**Amendment:** “The exact 100-family benchmark mean is a finite-set descriptive quantity. Family intervals are approximate model-based uncertainty for hypothetical independent families from this fixed authoring/review process, with the observed topic mix and equal-family weights; they do not cover new authors, training corpora, seeds or backbones. Pooled family resampling assumes independent exchangeable family records; shared-template dependence is a limitation, not corrected by N=100. Report known construction/lineage dependence from existing metadata without reopening test content before the access gate. The intervention is the complete training-rendering recipe: switching, lexical choice, tokenization and length change together; Δ_C is not an isolated causal effect of code-switching.”

## 4. Should-fix — bootstrap reproducibility and the S3 bounded interval are underspecified

**Quote (§7):** “Python `random.Random(101)`, one index sequence shared by all estimands”; “95% percentile interval”; “Hoeffding bounded interval (radius ≈ 27.2 pp at N = 100)”.
**Finding:** `choices` and repeated `randrange` give different seeded samples; row ordering and quantile interpolation can change discrete boundary labels. S3 family differences lie in `[-2,2]`, so its Hoeffding radius is 54.3 pp, not 27.2. The plan's “same analysis” clause otherwise invites copying the primary radius everywhere.
**Amendment:** “Sort records lexicographically by family ID. With a fresh `random.Random(101)`, form 10,000 rows of 100 draws using `rng.randrange(100)` in row-major order; reuse this matrix for all endpoints, contrasts and seed-specific intervals. Sort bootstrap estimates and use linear quantiles: h=(9999)p, interpolate sorted entries floor(h) and ceil(h), p=.025/.975. Record Python version and matrix hash. Wald uses sample SD (`ddof=1`) and z=1.959963984540054. Hoeffding radius is `(b−a)*sqrt(log(40)/(2N))`, intersected with `[a,b]`: `[-1,1]` for accuracy differences and `[-2,2]` for S3. Wilson intervals apply separately to each checkpoint's N binary outcomes, never pooled 3N seed rows. Crossed resampling, if shown, uses 10,000 draws with independent family and paired-seed indices (both with replacement), seed 102, all arms/forms kept together, and the same quantiles; it remains sensitivity-only.”

## 5. Blocking — the secondary inferential escape clause is not a complete testing contract

**Quote (§7):** “If any secondary is claimed inferentially, Holm at α = .05 applies”; “one-sided p = #{θ* ≤ −2 pp}/10,000”; “All endpoints (strict, v1, v2) get the same analysis”.
**Finding:** These empirical-bootstrap tail proportions are not finite-sample calibrated null p-values; percentile inversion can be an approximate procedure, but null-boundary calibration (especially sparse/degenerate retention) is absent. Holm cannot supply missing p-value validity. The three-element family omits additional retention comparators, Δ_R, numeric endpoints and per-seed tests, while later prose invites claims from all of them. Choosing whether to invoke inference after seeing outputs is avoidable.
**Amendment:** “Δ_C on strict accuracy is the sole confirmatory estimand. All secondary contrasts, numeric endpoints and seed-specific McNemar p-values are descriptive; no confirmatory secondary claim or Holm analysis will be made. Retention labels describe the prespecified interval criterion only, not multiplicity-controlled non-inferiority. Delete the conditional Holm/p-value clause. Any future inferential secondary family requires a pre-output amendment listing every hypothesis, direction, endpoint and comparator plus a validated testing procedure; after access it is post hoc.”

## 6. Blocking — retention categories overlap

**Quote (§8):** '"retained" only if L(Ret_E) > −2; "degraded" if U(Ret_E) < 0; "retention trade-off" if Δ_C is Support/Useful support and U(Ret_E) < −2'.
**Finding:** `[-1.5,-0.5]` satisfies both retained and degraded; a supported Δ_C with `[-4,-3]` satisfies degraded and trade-off. Non-inferiority and a small loss can legitimately coexist, but one mutually exclusive label cannot express the present rules. §6 extends labels to five comparators; §8 explicitly repeats only two.
**Amendment:** “For each of the five §6 English contrasts, report separate descriptive flags: `within-margin criterion met` iff L>−2 pp; `loss detected` iff U<0; `loss beyond margin` iff U<−2 pp. Otherwise the respective criterion is not established. Print all applicable flags together. Add `retention trade-off` only to EC−EE or EC−unchanged when the primary label is Support/Useful support and that comparator has U<−2. Equality at −2 or 0 does not satisfy a strict inequality. The degeneracy override in amendment 2 takes precedence.”

A versioned planning rerun uses DEV k/40 with the original q0/delta grid, explicitly reports infeasible cells, and handles p0=1 as zero-headroom: only q0=0, delta=0 is admissible and degenerate. Do not change the design or labels from this rerun.”

## 8. Should-fix — 60 updates are defensible, but the smoke result is not an overfitting diagnosis

**Quote (§3):** “3 passes × 20 updates = **60 updates**”; D043: “replacing the provisional 256, which the D042 probe showed is in the memorisation regime”.
**Finding:** The probe used 16 families, 32 updates and constant LR; near-zero training loss is not measured held-out harm. Extrapolating by passes to ten times the data and a new decay schedule does not establish that 256 overfits or 60 avoids it. No tuning on DEV is needed to make 60 a legitimate fixed engineering choice.
**Amendment:** “Retain 60 updates and the stated LR formula as a conservative, resource-feasible design choice made before Phase-2 outputs, not a validated optimum or guarantee against overfitting. The constant-LR 16-family smoke established numerical feasibility and rapid target fitting only. The 160-family/decayed-LR recipe changes both data scale and optimization. Report possible undertraining and overtraining as limitations; neither loss curves nor DEV may extend, shorten or select the schedule.”

## 9. Optional — make the paired data-order rationale and encoding explicit

**Quote (§3):** “each family contributes its E example then its arm-specific second example, consecutively; updates take the next 16 examples (8 families)”.
**Finding:** This is implementable and useful: every accumulation window has matched families and targets across arms. It is not ordinary random shuffling of 320 independent examples; only eight distinct mathematical families contribute per update. Matching initialization does not imply matching dropout draws across variable-length prompts.
**Amendment:** “Hash the UTF-8 bytes of `phase2-D043:{s}:{p}:{family_id}` with decimal s and p, no quotes/newline; break hash ties by family ID. Freeze and hash all three per-seed ordered exposure lists before training. Consecutive pairs deliberately preserve content balance in each accumulation window; there is no optimizer step between the two examples. The effective content batch is eight families. Pairing controls initial adapter tensors and exposure order, not necessarily dropout randomness or equal input-token compute.”

## 10. Blocking — ‘60 updates’ has no defined meaning when GradScaler skips

**Quote (§3):** “The adapter after update 60 is the evaluated checkpoint”; “per-update loss, gradient norm, lr, scaler skips”.
**Finding:** A skipped optimizer step can coexist with finite loss. Counting 60 attempted windows gives fewer updates; repeating a window or continuing to 60 successful steps changes exposures and the schedule. The smoke loop advances its cursor even on a skip; logging alone does not resolve the contract.
**Amendment:** “A valid run consists of exactly 60 accumulation windows and 60 successful optimizer steps over exactly three passes. Set lr(u) before step u; unscale gradients before clipping. Any scaler skip, non-finite loss/gradient or non-finite saved parameter is a numerical failure; do not silently advance, replay or add exposure windows. Log it and apply amendment 11. Freeze the scaler growth/backoff/growth-interval settings and record actual attempted/successful steps and data cursors.”

## 11. Blocking — numerical/data failures are not infrastructure failures; reserve logic can select stable seeds

**Quote (§10):** “runtime disconnect or crash, OOM, failed save or reload, non-finite loss, hash or label-assertion failure”; “that seed is replaced by reserve seed **44**, then **55**”.
**Finding:** Hash/label failures cannot be repaired by a different seed; non-finite training behavior can depend on the treatment. Automatically replacing it selects successful training outcomes. Reserve reuse, reserve retry limits and allocation when two original seeds fail are unspecified.
**Amendment:** “Distinguish transient infrastructure interruption from numerical failure and protocol/data assertion failure. Only documented transient infrastructure failure permits one from-scratch retry with identical code/configuration/seed. After its second failure replace the entire seed triplet using the next unused seed in the global queue [44,55], processing original slots in order [11,22,33]; each reserve has the same two-attempt maximum per arm and is never reused. Keep already successful arms of an otherwise valid triplet; discard the whole triplet only when replacing its seed. Numerical or hash/label failure halts the study for diagnosis, not seed substitution; any repair requires a dated amendment before further training and discloses all existing outputs. Never quietly overwrite a failed attempt or change stack/configuration under an ‘identical’ retry.”

## 16. Should-fix — power summaries overstate calibration and misstate precision

**Quote (§9):** “Median 95% half-width ≈ 2.0–5.0 pp (2.1–2.9 at the ceiling)”; “cannot establish a useful (≥ 5 pp) gain or 2 pp English non-inferiority”.
**Finding:** The stored grid spans 1.97–5.64 pp, and ceiling cases span 2.06–3.64; the narrower quoted ranges mostly describe null scenarios. Power is Wald-based; bootstrap validation covers only two +5-pp alternatives with 300 datasets, not null size, retention boundaries or coverage. ‘Cannot’ confuses low power with impossibility. “Gain-only” raises success probabilities, but the simulator independently draws arms and still permits realized losses.
**Amendment:** “Planning estimates use Wald intervals under a specific independent-family, conditionally independent-arm/seed model with nonnegative family probability changes; individual outcomes can worsen. Cross-model discordance is only a proxy. Across the stored feasible grid, median half-width is 1.97–5.64 pp (2.06–3.64 at p0=.943). Two +5-pp bootstrap comparisons support a limited shortcut check, not calibrated 95% coverage or all decision rules. Precision is scenario-dependent, not promised. Useful-gain support and retention can occur but are not reliably powered; a boundary effect of exactly +5 pp should rarely yield L>+5. Archive versioned calibration of the final implemented interval under null, asymmetric, sparse/degenerate and margin-boundary cases before outputs; retain unfavorable cases without selecting a favorable method.”

## 17. Should-fix — qualitative auditing needs a frozen denominator and adjudication protocol

**Quote (§12):** “a two-AI-tagger audit plus human check of all E+R and E+C failures on C (format-only vs substantive; descriptive, never overwriting strict scores)”.
**Finding:** Tagger identity, blinding, disagreement resolution, substantive categories and what ‘human check’ covers are unspecified. Conditioning on failures supports error composition, not an unbiased causal mechanism comparison; success cases can also contain bad reasoning.
**Amendment:** “Before outputs, freeze the audit rubric/prompts, tagger model revisions/settings and deterministic adjudication rules. After all evaluation completes, audit every strict-incorrect C output of every retained ER/EC seed, preserving checkpoint/seed IDs in records but blinding arm labels to taggers where feasible. Both AI taggers work independently; a human checks every audited output and resolves disagreements under the rubric, with unresolved cases labelled unresolved. Report failure counts over all C generations and category counts over audited failures, agreement and uncertainty; make no mechanism or all-output reasoning-validity claim from failure-only auditing. Never replace primary scores.”
