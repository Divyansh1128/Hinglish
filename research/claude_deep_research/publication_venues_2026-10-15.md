**ARR rules that affect this team** (https://aclrollingreview.org/cfp, read today):

- A Limitations section is mandatory and does not count toward the page limit. Papers without one are desk-rejected.
- An "Ethical considerations" section is optional, encouraged, and also outside the page limit.
- The Responsible NLP checklist is mandatory, and wrong answers can lead to desk rejection.
- Use of generative AI for writing or coding must be disclosed.
- From October 2026, a submission is guaranteed review only if it names a **qualified service contributor** (reviewer). Submissions without one go to a lottery.
  - Undergraduate authors probably do not qualify, so an ARR submission likely needs a faculty or PhD co-author who does.
  - It is unconfirmed whether the SRW direct-submission track applies the same rule. Check this with the SRW chairs.

## 2. Which venue fits which paper

- **Pilot only (Nov–Dec 2026):** submit to the **EACL 2027 SRW** as a short paper by **15 Dec 2026**. It is the only confirmed, open, student-eligible ACL venue whose deadline falls after the mid-November pilot.
  - A 30-family, one-model diagnostic study is too thin for the 4 Jan ARR cycle. Reviewers there would compare it with CodeMixBench and CodeMixTax, which evaluate many models.
- **Full training study (Mar–May 2027):** submit to **ARR in May 2027, aiming at EMNLP 2027**. The date is unconfirmed, so watch https://2027.emnlp.org/.
  - Fallbacks: the ACL 2027 SRW (if the deadline is mid-March or later), an ACL or EMNLP 2027 workshop such as CALCS, LoResLM or MRL if one runs, then ICON 2027.

## 3. What reviewers expect: model papers

| Model paper | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- |
| CodeMixBench (Yang & Chai, EMNLP 2025 main; https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.109/) | Includes a code-mixed GSM8K across 18 languages and releases its data. It sets the bar for evaluation breadth: many models and a public benchmark. |
| MathMist (Sobhani et al., Findings EACL 2026; https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.131/) | A parallel math benchmark that includes code-switched reasoning prompts, with data on GitHub. It sets the pattern for perturbation and prompt-condition analysis and per-language error analysis. |
| Anand et al., Hindi/English math (AAAI 2025; https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18415) | Closest training precedent. Reviewers will ask how E+R/E+C differs from bilingual curriculum tuning. Its own appendix says Romanized Hindi was excluded. |
| mCoT (Lai & Nissim, ACL 2024; https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.649.pdf) | Multilingual math SFT with cross-lingual consistency evaluation. It is the template for a training paper with paired evaluation. |
| "Competence Collapse in Code-Mixed Generation" (Yadav, LoResLM 2026; https://aclanthology.org/2026.loreslm-1.3/) | A single-author Hinglish workshop paper. Even at workshop level it evaluated nine model architectures, which shows that breadth over models is expected. |

Common expectations, mapped to this project:

1. **Structure:** motivation; a precise E/R/C definition and borrowing policy; data construction with human review; a setup that is fully specified (model revision, decoding, prompt, scorer); results; error analysis; Limitations; Ethics.
2. **Baselines:** for the pilot, the same items in E as the within-family reference. A **second and third unchanged open model of similar size** is cheap inference and would answer the obvious "only Qwen3-4B?" objection; this is a team decision. For training: E+E as the exposure control, plus the base model. A translate-C-to-E pipeline is optional.
3. **Statistics:** report n = 30 *families*, not 90 items. Use paired family-level tests (exact McNemar on discordant E/R and E/C pairs) and cluster-bootstrap CIs over families. Present the pilot as descriptive or diagnostic and make no confirmatory claims. For training: paired seeds (at least 3), an interval for the E+C − E+R difference, and an English-retention check.
4. **Dataset release:** reviewers expect the items, model outputs and scorer to be released.
   - The repo has **no licence yet**; choose one, for example CC BY 4.0 for data and MIT for code.
   - State that the drafts were AI-authored and human-reviewed.
   - Anonymize the files for submission. Cloud-drive links are not accepted.
5. **Ethics:** cover reviewer consent, compensation and exposure disclosures; AI-generated source texts; the Romanized-Hindi register and dialect coverage; and the ARR AI-assistance disclosure.
6. **Limitations:** one model; 30 families; single-author renderings, so style may be specific to one writer; script confound (Roman vs Devanagari not tested); token-length differences between E, R and C; the strict-scoring policy.

## 4. Concrete two-paper plan

### Paper 1: "Format and lexical confounds in Hinglish math evaluation"

The claim of Paper 1 is that prompt and output-format choices can create or hide E/R/C gaps, and that the remaining real R-specific errors are misread Hindi operation words. It is a 4-page short paper at the EACL 2027 SRW.

- **Positioning:** CodeMixTax and CodeMixBench already report E/R/C-style accuracy gaps, so the contribution is *not* "first Hinglish math gap". It is:
  - a human-reviewed triplet methodology;
  - a measured format and scoring confound;
  - an error taxonomy of operation-word errors (bhaag, bhinn).

**Timeline:**

| Date | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 15 Oct – 5 Nov | Collect exposure disclosures. Independent R01/R02 solves, adjudication by A01. Freeze texts, units and splits. **Before** the run, write down that the pilot uses both the original and the revised prompt, with the revised prompt as primary. |
| **6 Nov** | Send a draft to the SRW mentorship programme: introduction, method, analysis plan and placeholder tables. This is optional but valuable. |
| Mid-Nov | Run the reviewed pilot. Inspect every raw output. Have two people code the failure taxonomy. |
| 1 Dec | Decide archival vs non-archival (see below). |
| **15 Dec** | Submit. Notification 5 Jan; camera-ready 19 Jan; travel grant 21 Jan; present in March (virtually if allowed). |

Archival or non-archival:

- Choose **archival** if Paper 2 will cite Paper 1 and reuse the pilot only as motivation.
- Choose **non-archival** if the pilot results will be a core section of Paper 2.

**Must be true to submit Paper 1:**

- **Data and protocol**
  1. All 30 families are human-accepted and frozen, with hashes.
  2. The prompt rule, both prompt conditions and the scorer were fixed **before** outputs were seen.
- **Evidence**
  3. The format and Devanagari-drift findings are shown on the reviewed pilot itself. The 36 fixture prompts are excluded development evidence and can only be cited as having motivated the design.
  4. Raw outputs, the scorer and the data are packaged anonymously.
- **Writing and process**
  5. Limitations, Ethics and AI-use disclosure are written.
  6. A faculty co-author or advisor has signed off.
  7. A novelty re-check has been run against CodeMixTax, Indi-RomCoM and recent arXiv work.

### Paper 2: "Does code-mixed math adaptation add value beyond Romanized Hindi?"

Paper 2 compares E+E, E+R and E+C QLoRA fine-tuning under matched exposure, keeping the English solution supervision identical across arms. It is aimed at the ARR May 2027 cycle and EMNLP 2027 (unconfirmed), as a long paper or a strong short one.

**Timeline:**

| Date | Step |
| --- | --- |
| Dec–Jan | Approve the sample size and the practical effect and retention margins. Build training and held-out families, including independently authored test items. Pre-register the analysis in the repo. |
| Jan–Mar 2027 | Paired-seed training runs (3 arms × at least 3 seeds), then evaluation on held-out E/R/C. |
| Mid-Mar | If results are complete, consider the ACL 2027 SRW (call unconfirmed). Otherwise continue. |
| Apr – late May | Write the paper and run the ARR checklist. Submit to the ARR May cycle (exact date unconfirmed). Fallbacks in Jun–Sep: an ACL/EMNLP 2027 workshop, then ICON 2027. |

**Must be true to submit Paper 2:**

- **Data and splits**
  1. A frozen, family-grouped split ledger with no pilot or fixture descendants.
  2. A held-out-author C test, so a gain cannot simply be adaptation to one writer's style.
- **Training and analysis**
  3. Matched exposure, with tokens and runtime reported.
  4. Paired seeds and an interval for the E+C − E+R difference, with E+E as the control.
  5. English retention is measured.
  6. At least one second backbone or model size, or else an explicit Limitation explaining why not.
- **Release and process**
  7. A licence and release plan.
  8. An ARR-qualified service contributor among the authors.

**Rule for both papers:** a narrow null result is publishable if it is framed as evidence about data choice. A wide interval is not, so sample-size decisions must come before training.
