## 1. Claim, scope and four-page outline

Title options: (1) **Format and Scoring Confounds in Romanized-Hindi Math Evaluation**; (2) **From Answer Formatting to Operation-Word Misreading: An E/R/C Diagnostic Study**; (3) **Auditing Format and Lexical Errors in Hinglish Math Evaluation**.
Proposed claim: excluded development runs expose a prompt/scoring confound; after a disclosed protocol revision, two responses remain consistent with Romanized-Hindi operation-word misreading. A human-reviewed paired pilot will test how often these failure classes occur under the frozen protocol. This is not a “first Hinglish math gap,” a causal lexical finding, or a training result.
Current evidence: FMT2 strict-invalid outputs fell from 10/36 to 0/36; 8/36 v1 outputs had dimensionless suffix failures. R Devanagari incidence fell from 8/12 to 0/12; this script detector is not a complete English-compliance audit. The two v2 R errors concern `bhaag`/`bhinn`, but independent bilingual validation is still needed for a lexical interpretation.
Crucial qualification: both prompt and fixture version changed (one R ambiguity repaired, one alias added). Thus 10→0 is a development comparison, not an isolated prompt treatment effect. Strict/numeric disagreement alone is not “format-only”; the numeric endpoint can accept wrong units and reject equivalent converted quantities.
Keep E/R/C definitions explicit: English, Romanized Hindi, and Roman-script Hindi–English code mixing. Related-work positioning uses CodeMixBench, CodeMixTax, Sarvam-M and Indi-RomCoM with their script, provenance and publication-status differences. No claim that C≈E and R<E identifies vocabulary or excludes script/tokenization explanations.
